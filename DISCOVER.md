# Discover relevant judgment

## Start from the workspace

Use the entry point named in workspace instructions. When none is named, look for
`principles/index.md` and the domain documentation the workspace already references.
Reuse existing authoritative records. If there is no collection, work normally and
consider a record only when a real decision justifies one.

A repository, a product, and an operating team can have different scopes. Locate the
authority for this work. Cross-workspace links are references, not automatic adoption.
For a non-filesystem workspace, use the configured document or connector location;
identify any inaccessible records needed for the current choice.

## Follow meaning progressively

1. Read the small entry index and the authoritative records under its always-consult
   section. Keep this set limited to workspace-wide commitments.
2. Identify the concerns the task touches: whose decision it is, which concepts change,
   what is observed or derived, and which consumers rely on the result. Follow the
   index's applicability descriptions into the relevant scoped records.
3. Read those records, including status, authority, tradeoff, scope, and boundary
   examples. Follow evidence links when their details could change the decision.
4. Search a neighboring concern when the task crosses a boundary or the indexed
   vocabulary misses it. Search synonyms, references, and the actual domain behavior;
   do not assume a filename or lexical match establishes relevance.
5. Stop when the present choice and material risks have sufficient support. Expand
   again only when a finding changes the task's scope, meaning, or risk.

For example, a request about a summary export can implicate a principle about
preserving observations, even when no event-writing code is changing. Conversely, a
presentation-only date-format fix may not touch the meaning of an observation at all.

## Keep the kinds of knowledge distinct

- **Adopted commitment:** a human-owned rule or preference for future choices, with
  a scope and a traceable decision. Check its boundary before applying it.
- **Contextual decision:** a choice made for particular circumstances. Reuse its
  reasoning only after checking those circumstances; do not silently universalize it.
- **Observation:** behavior verified against a named artifact or experiment. It
  describes the implementation, not necessarily the intent.
- **Working explanation:** an inference with supporting evidence, uncertainty, and
  an indication of what would disprove it. Useful for investigation, never doctrine.

A proposed or retired principle is not an active commitment. An old index label does
not override the record's current status. Existing workspace policies may use a
different format: establish their actual authority instead of demanding migration.

## Missing context is a decision about risk

Do not reconstruct a missing principle from its title. If its content could affect
security, domain meaning, an external promise, or an irreversible action, hold that
part of the work and seek the missing evidence or authorized decision. Continue
unaffected investigation or reversible work. For immaterial gaps, state an assumption
when useful and proceed.

A broken link or absent search result is not evidence that no commitment exists.
Use one targeted fallback through existing documentation or known references. If the
remaining gap matters, name the evidence needed to resolve it.

If two records conflict, do not choose the newest or the most local solely by
location. Check whether a human-established precedence or explicit scoped exception
resolves them. Otherwise bring the concrete conflict to discussion.

Re-read relied-on records after a known change, a resumed session without their
context, or a material scope expansion. Keep implementation findings in work notes
until a human adopts a change to the commitment.
